## The old precondition was unreachable where it mattered

The shipped rule only judged movement on a frame whose shape is 'index extended, every other finger curled' (`MotionDecider.handshape`: index tip/MCP-to-PIP ratio >= 1.6 **and** all four others <= 1.20). Measured over the cached photographs, that gate opens on **0.0 % of J frames** and **22.7 % of Z frames**, while opening on 40.2 % of G, 17.8 % of L and 16.0 % of D - it is closed on the two letters it exists for and widest on three it must never claim. The display precondition below opens on 93.3 % of J and 89.4 % of Z. It also opens on the two readings it is built from - a real I on 95.2 % and a real X on 5.2 % - and refusing those is the trajectory rule's job, not the precondition's, which is why the fire columns below are the ones to read. Among every other letter measured none exceeds 0.5 %.

The two `shown as` columns are the argument for the change. The classifier has no J, so a real J is *displayed* as the closest thing it knows, and it does that consistently: the J row is shown as I on 93.3 % of its frames, the Z row as X on 89.4 %, and the two letters the model is being asked to double as (real I, real X) are shown that way on 95.0 % and 95.6 % - which is exactly why the display alone is not the answer and the trajectory still has to agree.

The `refused by the OLD flat ceiling` column is the round-7 argument, and it is measured against the ceiling as round 6 shipped it (`p1 < 0.9` for both routes), not against the rule now in force: 72.7 % of real J frames were thrown away before the trajectory was ever looked at, against 0.6 % of Z frames. That asymmetry is the whole reason the two routes are no longer treated alike. A confident X really is an X, so Z keeps its ceiling; a confident I is exactly what a J frame looks like, so confidence was never evidence against J, and the per-route table `DISPLAY_P1_MAX_FOR = {'J': None, 'Z': 0.9}` now says so. What protects a held I is not the ceiling but the trajectory: a still hand has no reversal, no net travel and no path, and `live.py --logic-test` pins exactly that, on a synthetic I held for 1.5 s at p1 0.99. Photographs cannot test it, because consecutive cached frames are different hands and their difference looks like movement - which is why the I row above nevertheless fires 23 J's over 625 frames here and is the one number in this file that has to be re-measured on a real hand before anyone calls the exemption free.

`window allows J / Z` is the share rule in force: the fraction of judged frames whose window majority named that letter, i.e. how often each letter could fire at all (3.3 % for J on J frames, 22.4 % for Z on Z frames). I frames are now judgable too - 16.2 % of them allow a J, against 0.5 % that allow a Z - and every other letter stays under 14.4 %. `refused` counts frames whose path matched while no precondition held; it guards against a reading that flickers between I and X, and on this data it never had to intervene - the flicker case is covered by a synthetic check in `live.py --logic-test` instead.

Percentages here are **frame-weighted** across datasets. An earlier version of this file averaged the per-dataset percentages instead, which let 30 cached J frames from one source count as much as 300 from another: on the round-6 rule that mean-of-means reported the J display gate as 47.3 % where the frames themselves said 23.6 %. Any percentage quoted from before that fix is a mean of means and is not comparable with the tables above.

## What changed in the code, and what did not

```python
MotionDecider.DISPLAY_FOR       = {'I': 'J', 'X': 'Z'}
MotionDecider.DISPLAY_P1_MAX    = 0.9   (default)
MotionDecider.DISPLAY_P1_MAX_FOR  = {'J': None, 'Z': 0.9}   # None = no ceiling
MotionDecider.DISPLAY_MIN_SHARE = 0.6
```

A frame now enters the trajectory rule if the shape gate holds **or** the classifier's top-1 is I at any confidence (candidate J) or an X below that route's confidence ceiling (candidate Z), and the letter that may fire is the one named on at least 60% of the frames in the window the rule actually judges. The shape gate is unchanged and still on its own opens both letters, so nothing that fired before can fail to fire now. What the trajectory requires did not move: same `WINDOW`, `MIN_HOLD`, `SEG`, `J_*`, `Z_*`, and `--calibrate-jz` still cannot tighten anything, only loosen. `update()` called without a reading is byte-for-byte the old rule, which is what `live.py --logic-test` drives.


## What this did not fix, in one line each

* Z fired 1 times locally and 0 on the MSL clips, in spite of the precondition now being reachable on Z (89.4 % of local Z frames, 35.1 % of MSL Z frames). The gate is not what is stopping Z: the shipped zig-zag thresholds (`Z_PATH`, `Z_DROP`, two tip reversals, hand still) matched nothing measured, because they were written against a synthetic zig-zag. That is what `--calibrate-jz` is for, and with a reachable gate it can now measure a real Z stroke at all.

* J fires rarely - 62 times over 15772 local frames, 3 over 566 MSL frames. Same cause, same remedy.

* Two kinds of false fire, and they have different causes. The shape gate ones predate everything: G 40.2 %, L 17.8 %, Z 22.7 %, D 16.0 %, X 7.5 % of frames open a gate the rule then reads as a hook, because between two different people's photographs any difference looks like movement. Tightening that gate would break letters that fire correctly today, so it is recorded as the next job rather than quietly changed. The new ones are the I route's: non-J/Z letters went 15 -> 38 locally, and on the MSL clips, which are continuous strokes rather than photo jumps, the same route changed the false fires from 12 to 12. On a real hand the discriminator is the trajectory, not the reading - a held I has no wrist reversal, no net travel and no path, which `live.py --logic-test` checks - but only the webcam can say whether an I that arrives with a wobble types J, and this file has never seen one.


## What this does not prove

No webcam frame was used, and no number in this file is a live accuracy. Only `python src/live.py --selftest` and using the app measure the real camera. The local blocks are sequences of different hands, so a fire here does not mean the stroke was right - only that the gate can be reached at all, and the honest way to read the two 'old -> new' columns is as a reachability change, not a precision change. The precision of the *precondition* itself is a separate measurement and lives in the per-letter 'shown as' columns above.
